The first 90 days, in the order that matters

Most newcomers hear “register your car” and stop there. Colorado actually runs three clocks side by side. Start the one with the shortest fuse and work down.

  1. Days 1 to 30: driver license. The Colorado DMV says to transfer an out-of-state license within 30 days of establishing residency. Book early, because a visit to a license office needs an appointment. See the document list.
  2. Days 1 to 90: insurance, then title and registration. You need proof of active Colorado auto insurance to register. Colorado’s minimum liability limits are $25,000 / $50,000 for injury and $15,000 for property damage per accident (DMV).
  3. Before you register: emissions, if your address needs it. A vehicle that will be registered in the Denver metro or North Front Range program area may need an Air Care Colorado inspection first. How the age exemption works.

Quick answers

How long do I have to get a Colorado driver license after moving?

The Colorado DMV says to transfer your license within 30 days of establishing residency, and an appointment is required.

How long do I have to register my car in Colorado?

The Colorado DMV says to title and register a vehicle within 90 days of establishing residency, at your county motor vehicle office.

Do I need an emissions test when I move to the Front Range?

Possibly. Air Care Colorado says a vehicle brought into the Denver metro or North Front Range program area from another state must pass an inspection before it is registered, unless it is new enough to be exempt.
