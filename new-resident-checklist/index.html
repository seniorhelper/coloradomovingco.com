The new-resident checklist

Two deadlines, one insurance rule and a stack of forms. Here is the order that keeps you out of late fees.

Everything below comes from the Colorado Division of Motor Vehicles’ new-resident page, read on 11 November 2026. Offices set their own appointment systems and fees, so check yours.

Driver license: 30 days

  • Transfer your license within 30 days of establishing residency.
  • An appointment is required. Pre-register online first, then book.
  • Bring approved identification and proof of address, plus your previous state’s license (valid, or expired less than one year) or a motor vehicle record dated within the last 30 days.
  • Names must match across documents, or you need paperwork showing the change.
  • Applicants under 18 need a parent or guardian to sign form DR 2460, or bring it notarized.
  • Expect to take any required tests and pay the fee; your old license will be hole-punched.

Vehicle title and registration: 90 days

You title and register at your county motor vehicle office, not at a state license office. Appointments for these usually cannot be booked online, though some counties take walk-ins.

Source: dmv.colorado.gov/new-to-colorado.

Late fees are real. Adams County’s new-resident guide reports a $25-a-month late fee, capped at $100, for missing the 90-day window (Adams County, Welcome to Colorado PDF). Check your own county before assuming there is a grace period.

A sensible order

  1. Buy Colorado insurance before you arrive; many carriers let you start a policy on a future date.
  2. Book the license appointment the week you land.
  3. Get the VIN verification and, if needed, the emissions test (details here) on the same errand run.
  4. Visit the county office with every form printed and the title in hand.

General information, not legal advice. Confirm current fees and forms at dmv.colorado.gov or with your county clerk.
